      How it works

      1. Each round blinks a patch of sky imaged by SPHEREx over hours or days.
      2. Stars stay still. Click anything that moves.
      3. Practice rounds hide a planted fake mover so you can train. Fakes get fainter as you improve.
      4. Real rounds are untouched data. Your clicks go to the Candidates board, weighted by your practice skill.

      A real Planet X is probably too faint for SPHEREx. What you will really catch: asteroids, comets and distant icy bodies. The method is the same one astronomers use.

      About SkyShift

      NASA's SPHEREx telescope photographs the entire sky every six months in 102 colours of near-infrared light. Things that move between pictures (asteroids, comets, nearby stars, perhaps even an unknown planet) give themselves away by shifting position.

      A map app lets you zoom through space. SkyShift lets you zoom through time:

      Time zoomWhat movesTypical motion
      HoursMain-belt asteroids~6 pixels per hour
      DaysDistant icy bodies, a "Planet X"1-12 pixels per day
      MonthsCompare whole survey passes-
      YearFast nearby stars~2 pixels per year

      The slower it moves, the farther away it is.

      Honest notes

      • SPHEREx sees each spot through a filter whose colour changes across the detector. SkyShift only blinks images taken at matching colours, except in Hours mode, where frames are brightness-balanced because motion is what matters.
      • Very bright objects can leave red and blue patterns in Difference mode even when nothing changed. These are false changes from slightly different image sharpness.
      • Practice fakes in Hunt mode are clearly labelled and never reach the Candidates board.
